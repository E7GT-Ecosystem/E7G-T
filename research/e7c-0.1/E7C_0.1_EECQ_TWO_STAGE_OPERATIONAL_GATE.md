# EEC-Q two-stage operational relation (provisional review candidate)

The pure all-list mathematical layer was merged in PR #102 (merge commit
`fb51c34a33ae2cbbbfb73663b8b3e1218541cbb9`). Published experimental
v0.12.1/RGP2 remains the source; the selected predicate, source and IR
editions remain those of PR #101.

`E7CEECQTwoStageOperational.lean` now has two distinct constructions:
`plan` is the complete specification trace, while `run` uses a cursor and
fuelled traversal. At each attempt or row it checks the step bound first,
charges the step, checks ledger capacity, and only then advances the cursor.
The cursor carries unvisited first-stage rows and accumulated retained and
excluded rows. Second-stage rows come from completed first-stage retained
rows, in original order. Zero steps never visit a row. A failed ledger append
consumes the attempted step and adds no event. Resource termination carries
only progress, never a partial `Partition`. Unsupported and undetermined
policy results follow their respective recorded attempt events.

**Established Lean laws in this revision:** `zero_steps_never_traverse` and
`failed_first_append_consumes_step` cover the boundary behavior at entry.
The pure all-list theorem of PR #102 remains independent of budgets.

**Open proof obligations, required before acceptance:** for every admitted
finite row list and policy, prove the staged traversal's success result is
the pure three-way partition when both bounds suffice; prove its event ledger
is the appropriate prefix of independent `plan` on exhaustion and its exact
step count includes the failed append; prove the stage-specific exclusion
progress fields agree with that prefix. No theorem about `cut plan` alone
establishes these traversal properties. Adversarial boundary examples must
cover exhaustion at each stage, duplicate rows, signed rational coefficients,
and same marginals with different correlated Joints. Until these are proved
and reviewed, PR #103 has no semantic acceptance claim.

The Python tests independently enumerate finite source replay and IR
observations under budgets and policies, but do not establish the general
Python-to-Lean relation. The later bridge requires a bijective encoding of
admitted graph tags and canonical rows into Lean `Graph`/`Row`; exact rational
normalization and ordered Joint support equality; edition and policy
agreement; ledger row-key serialization; child witness and IR-package
admission; and Python/IR refinement of the Lean staged evaluator for all
admitted inputs. No published source, B1/B2/B3 grammar, profile adoption or
product API changes here.
