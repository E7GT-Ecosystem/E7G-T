# S1 constructive exact collision collection

Status: `bounded_first_party_candidate; internal_fraction_representation`.

Base: main `63a2e4dc4c944fb0e523b086c95dcda28c859828`.
Source: E7G-T v0.12.1/RGP2 §§X.2–X.6; pinned FG3/0.1 example.
Proof vehicle: provisional Lean 4.34.0.

`proof-packages/lean-core/E7CS1ExactCollect.lean` represents an exact
rational coefficient by an integer numerator and strictly positive natural
denominator. Addition is the unreduced cross product
`(n₁d₂ + n₂d₁)/(d₁d₂)`. Zero is decided by its numerator. The finite
collector retains each exact target graph once, adds coefficients on a
collision and removes the graph when the total vanishes. This follows the
selected EEC-Q formal-state rule at the level of unreduced exact fractions;
it neither approximates coefficients nor treats them as probabilities.

Lean proves positive-denominator construction, cancellation of a fraction
with its opposite at a shared graph, and retention of a nonzero same-graph
sum. A concrete proof checks that `1/2` and `−2/4` cancel when their
contributions hit the same graph. The general lemmas range over arbitrary
integer numerators and positive denominators. The hosted axiom audit must find no new allowance
for the collector or its theorems.

The result is an internal representation. It is not yet a canonical sorted
state or reduced fraction encoding. This package does not prove associativity
and order independence of the finite collector, uniqueness of target graphs
for all inputs, equivalence to Lean's built-in `Rat` addition, admission of
raw input rows, the bridge from the previous Lean pre-collection traversal,
or refinement to Python's `Fraction`/FG3 implementation. Those are the next
proof and codec gates. No E7C-T017 or full calculus adequacy claim follows,
and no source/profile/product/schema/data/API migration occurs.

## Finite-list invariant extension (PR #65)

The next bounded step proves `insert_zeroFree`, `foldl_zeroFree` and
`collect_zeroFree`: after any finite list of graph-keyed contributions, every
retained coefficient has a nonzero numerator. Intermediate zero totals are
removed, including a collision that cancels an earlier retained amount.
`Equivalent` specifies internal fraction value equality by cross
multiplication; reflexivity, symmetry and the concrete `−2/4 = −1/2` case
are checked. Transitivity, compatibility of addition with equivalence,
uniqueness of all retained graph keys, ordering, normalization and source
correspondence remain separate proof gates. The internal rational representation
is not a canonical wire format.

## Graph-key uniqueness extension (PR #66)

`keyAbsent` states that a graph does not occur in a collected list, and
`uniqueKeys` requires each retained graph to be absent from the remaining
list. The insertion proof preserves absence for every other graph and proves
uniqueness when the input state is unique. Induction over the finite input
list proves `collect_uniqueKeys` for arbitrary contributions. Alongside
`collect_zeroFree`, this shows that every output graph occurs once with a
nonzero coefficient. The list is not proved sorted or independent of input
order; rational equivalence laws, canonical serialization, input admission
and the FG3/Python bridge remain open.

## Fraction equivalence extension (PR #67)

Cross-product equivalence is proved transitive for all internal fractions;
the intermediate denominator is positive and therefore cancellable in
integer arithmetic. With the earlier reflexive and symmetric lemmas this
establishes an equivalence relation on internal fraction values. The
collector's exact addition is also commutative up to this equivalence.
These facts do not yet establish addition congruence, associativity,
permutation independence of collected values, canonical normalization, or
the external FG3/Python bridge.

## Addition respects equivalent fractions (PR #68)

The integer cross-product proof establishes that replacing the left
coefficient by an equivalent unreduced fraction leaves the sum equivalent.
Commutativity and transitivity then give the same property for the right
coefficient and for replacing both inputs at once. This is a value-level
congruence proof for internal exact addition. It does not prove that the
collector's list representation is independent of insertion order or that
its output uses a canonical fraction encoding. Addition associativity,
zero-test compatibility with value equivalence, the finite permutation
theorem and the FG3/Python bridge remain separate gates.

## Associativity and zero compatibility (PR #69)

For three internal fractions, Lean proves that the two groupings of exact
addition have the same numerator and denominator, hence equivalent values.
It also proves that cross-product-equivalent fractions have zero numerators
simultaneously, because both denominators are nonzero. The executable
`isZero` decision therefore agrees across equivalent representations.
It uses the constructive decision procedure for integer equality, retaining
the original axiom allowance.
These are algebraic prerequisites for a finite collected-value permutation
theorem. The list-level theorem, canonical wire encoding and external
FG3/Python correspondence remain open.

## Finite-list value order theorem (PR #70)

For any graph, `observed` reads its retained coefficient or exact zero when
absent. The insertion lemma relates that observation to adding the incoming
row's coefficient only when the graph matches. A fold induction connects
the actual `collect` implementation to a per-graph exact sum. A separate
permutation induction, including arbitrary adjacent swaps, proves that
every graph's observed value is equivalent after any finite reordering of
input rows. Earlier uniqueness and nonzero invariants apply to both outputs.

The theorem compares values by cross multiplication. It does not assert
identical list order, identical unreduced numerator/denominator pairs,
canonical serialization, admission of raw source rows, or agreement with
Python's `Fraction` and FG3 implementation. Those are later gates.

## Selected FG3 codec bridge (candidate)

`E7CS1FG3Codec.lean` maps the existing Lean `Rat` pre-collection rows to
positive-denominator collector fractions and transports the finite-list
permutation theorem through that mapping. It proves round trips for fraction
wire pairs and for the 16 registered graph combinations: AB/AC/BC presence
with either no tag or tag `"marked"` (represented by Lean token `0`). Its
graph decoder validates edge labels, ignores duplicate edges and rejects
other tags. The Python-side selected test checks those combinations and
specific cancellation and collection examples against the actual S1 codec.

This bridge does not prove equality of Lean `Rat` and collector arithmetic,
Python `Fraction` refinement, the 512-bit input limit, reduced/sorted
canonical encoding, general Python string tags, or multi-row source
correspondence. Those require separate admission and proof gates. EEC-Q,
SF, CFS, RGP and WPC keep their existing dispositions; the optional REC
draft is not promoted. Published v0.12.1/RGP2 remains the baseline.

## Finite admitted-row collection bridge (candidate)

`E7CS1RowsCollect.lean` links the finite strict scan to exact collection.
For arbitrary finite support rows whose graph tags agree, and every
permutation of those rows, it proves that both resource-bounded scans
succeed with their ordered graph images, collected results have no zero
coefficients or duplicate graph keys, and every target graph's collected
coefficient is equivalent by exact cross multiplication. This composes
earlier separate scan and collector theorems under explicit admission and
resource premises; it does not equate the two list encodings.

The general bridge between Lean's normalized `Rat.add` and unreduced
collector addition remains open. The standard Lean rational lemmas used in
an initial proof carried `Classical.choice`, which is outside this package's
axiom allowance. Python `Fraction` refinement, reduced/sorted wire encoding
and full FG3 source adequacy also remain separate gates.

## Edition-bound graph tag registry (candidate)

`E7CS1TagRegistry.lean` replaces the fixed one-string example with an
explicit tag codec interface. Any registered Lean token may map to a distinct
FG3 string, provided the codec supplies a proved left inverse; a missing
token cannot encode and an unregistered string cannot decode. The round-trip
theorem covers every graph with an admitted token and all eight combinations
of the AB/AC/BC edges. The actual Python FG3 codec accepts arbitrary string
tags, so its broader admission is preserved and tested, not silently
identified with a particular Lean registry edition.

This is a conditional registry law. It does not construct a complete registry
for all Python strings, prove Python refinement, or change source editions.
