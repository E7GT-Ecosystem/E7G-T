# E7C/0.1 foundation build mandate

## 0. Mandate to the implementing AI

You are to begin constructing **E7C/0.1 — the E7 Constructive Calculus** as a
new, explicitly bounded research foundation beneath E7G-T.

Your task is not to add another descriptive profile to the kernel. Your task is
to define and implement a shared typed term language, semantics, effect system,
proof obligations, witness format and intermediate representation into which
selected bounded fragments of the existing E7G-T profiles can be translated.

Treat the following as governing constraints:

1. **E7G-T v0.12.1 remains the published experimental canonical reference.**
2. E7C/0.1 is not v0.15, v1.0 or a replacement kernel.
3. v0.13/WPC, v0.14/REC and MSC/0.1 remain optional research candidates or
   proposals with their existing identities and boundaries.
4. Do not change an existing profile merely to simplify E7C. Define an adapter,
   document a mismatch, or leave the feature outside the first executable
   fragment.
5. Use established mathematics for the metatheory. Originality may lie in the
   selected primitives, their composition and their application—not in
   avoiding precise definitions or known proof methods.
6. Every formal rule must be linked to at least one positive case, one negative
   or boundary case, and an implementation or explicit non-implementation
   disposition.
7. Every executable result must terminate in a declared outcome and, where the
   profile requires it, a replayable witness. No uncontrolled traceback,
   implicit coercion, silent truncation or hidden fallback is conformant.
8. Mathematical consistency, bounded conformance, empirical correctness,
   product value and external-system instantiation are different claims. Never
   report one as another.
9. Work through reviewable GitHub pull requests. Do not rewrite the canonical
   kernel, delete predecessor material or combine milestone work into a single
   unreviewable change.
10. Stop and record a design decision whenever two plausible semantics would
    produce observably different programs. Do not choose silently.

The implementation should be ambitious but incremental. A small language with
sound, inspectable semantics is preferable to a nominally comprehensive system
whose operators are only prose labels.

## 1. Strategic objective

E7G-T presently provides valuable but distributed mathematical structures:

- exact rational formal states and typed extension in EEC-Q;
- symbolic families and realisation in SF;
- family-of-state semantics in CFS;
- quotation, generation, source-retaining encoding and recursion in RGP;
- whole–part constitution and representation in WPC;
- finite claim–evidence information flow and witnesses in REC;
- local-to-global compatible families and obstructions in MSC;
- inherited projection, reconstruction, temporal and operational-phase
  disciplines in the constitutional kernel.

The missing foundation is one language in which these structures can be typed,
composed, evaluated, compared and lowered to executable programs without
silently identifying their different meanings.

E7C/0.1 must answer five questions:

1. What are the well-formed E7 terms, declarations and programs?
2. What mathematical object does each well-formed term denote?
3. How does a bounded program evaluate, including failure and uncertainty?
4. Which equations and rewrites preserve observable behaviour?
5. How are existing profiles embedded, interpreted or rejected?

The implementing AI must begin from the following explicit status map. A higher
version number, a merged proposal or an open PR does not change another
artifact's disposition.

## 2. Definition of success

E7C/0.1 succeeds as a research foundation only when an independent reviewer can:

1. read a finite grammar and typing judgement;
2. type-check representative programs without consulting unwritten intent;
3. evaluate the executable fragment deterministically;
4. distinguish identity, denotational equality, isomorphism, observational
   equivalence and inquiry-relative phase equivalence;
5. replay an evaluation witness using a separately implemented checker;
6. translate at least one bounded example from EEC-Q, WPC, MSC and REC;
7. reproduce positive, negative and resource-bound outcomes;
8. verify the stated initial metatheorems or identify a precise counterexample;
9. run at least one end-to-end application pilot; and
10. confirm that no canonical-kernel or product claim follows automatically.

Passing first-party tests alone is not completion. It is one gate in the build.

## 3. Intended mathematical architecture

E7C should be presented as an original typed calculus with a conventional,
auditable metatheory.

| Concern | Preferred mathematical machinery | Initial E7C use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terms and contexts | many-sorted typed calculus; limited dependent indices | signatures, carriers, editions, scopes and interfaces |
| Exact construction | free algebraic structures and finitely supported modules | EEC-Q formal states and canonicalisation |
| Composition | symmetric monoidal/category-theoretic structure where justified | independent composition and typed maps |
| Partiality | explicit result/effect semantics; restriction-category interpretation where useful | domain failure and partial maps |
| Equivalence | setoids, quotients and congruences | operational phases and observational equality |
| Indexed families | dependent families, indexed sets and symbolic constraints | SF and CFS |
| Local-to-global structure | finite diagrams, cones, limits and presheaf/sheaf-like compatibility | WPC and MSC |
| Change through time | labelled transition systems and trace semantics | histories and operation order |
| Recursion | initial algebras, coalgebras and guarded or bounded fixed points | RGP |
| Evidence states | product information orders or bilattice-like semantics | REC |
| Compilation | typed intermediate representation and semantics-preserving lowering | E7-IR |

These are candidate semantic tools, not mandatory branding. Use a structure only
where its axioms match the E7 operation. Do not describe an arbitrary diagram as
a category, an arbitrary local family as a sheaf, or a repeated computation as
a fixed point without satisfying the corresponding definitions.

## 4. Core design principles

### 4.1 Anti-collapse

The calculus must not silently identify:

- entity, description and configuration;
- configuration and formal state;
- source and view;
- view and source-retaining encoding;
- whole and materialised representation;
- exact identity and isomorphism;
- denotational equality and observational equivalence;
- observational equivalence and operational phase;
- family membership and formal coefficient;
- support/refutation and truth/falsity;
- failure, unsupported capability, uncertainty and resource exhaustion;
- scope order, construction rank, authority, time and importance.

Where an explicit conversion is valid, represent it as a typed operation with a
named policy and observable outcome.

### 4.2 Conservative profile integration

An E7C adapter is conservative when it preserves the source profile's admitted
terms, distinctions and results within the declared fragment. Translation into
E7C must not add equations that the source profile rejects.

Each adapter must state one of:

- `faithful_embedding`;
- `faithful_interpretation_with_explicit_loss`;
- `partial_translation`;
- `unsupported`.

### 4.3 Bounded executable core, extensible abstract calculus

The abstract calculus may describe countable or symbolic structures when their
semantics are clear. E7C-B1/0.1, the first implementation, must operate over a
finite and explicitly resource-bounded fragment. Symbolic or unbounded objects
must not be enumerated accidentally.

### 4.4 Errors are semantic results

The minimum outcome family is:

```text
success(value, witness)
invalid_input(diagnostic)
type_error(diagnostic)
domain_error(diagnostic)
unsupported(capability)
undetermined(obligation)
resource_limit(limit, progress)
```

The design must decide whether `invalid_input` is outside evaluation while the
remaining outcomes belong to the operational semantics. Record this decision
before proving progress.

### 4.5 Effects must be visible

At minimum, the type-and-effect system must be able to record:

- partiality;
- representational loss;
- dependence on an inquiry or equivalence criterion;
- access restriction;
- authority requirement;
- nondeterminism or retained alternatives;
- evidence dependence;
- temporal/history dependence;
- resource bounds; and
- external/domain bridge obligations.

Do not require every term to carry every effect. Define a finite effect row or
graded annotation with explicit composition rules.

## 5. Initial formal nucleus

### 5.1 Judgements

Begin with the principal judgement:

```math
\Delta;\Gamma;\Phi \vdash t : A \triangleright \varepsilon
```

where:

- `Delta` is the immutable profile, signature and edition environment;
- `Gamma` is the variable and resource context;
- `Phi` is the set of admitted constraints or proof obligations;
- `t` is a term;
- `A` is its type; and
- `epsilon` is its effect annotation.

Define separate judgements for:

```math
\Delta \vdash A\;\mathrm{type}
```

```math
\Delta;\Gamma \vdash \Phi\;\mathrm{admissible}
```

```math
\Delta;\Gamma;\Phi \vdash t \Downarrow o
```

```math
\Delta \vdash t \equiv u : A
```

```math
\Delta;Q \vdash t \approx u : A
```

The last two judgements must remain distinct: definitional/denotational equality
is not inquiry-relative phase equivalence.

### 5.2 Initial types

The first design must consider, and either admit or defer, these type families:

```text
Entity[K]
Config[Sigma]
State[R, Sigma]
Joint[A1, ..., An]
Map[A, B, P]
Rule[A, B, D]
Family[P, A]
Quote[rank, A]
View[A, V, policy]
Encoding[A, F, retention]
Whole[W]
Portion[W, member]
Scope[S]
Diagram[S]
CompatibleFamily[D]
Phase[A, Q]
History[A, clock]
Claim[C]
Evidence[E]
InformationStatus[L]
Witness[J]
Outcome[A, epsilon]
```

`R` is the coefficient structure for formal states. EEC-Q selects exact
rationals; the core must not assume that all states use rationals.

### 5.3 Initial term constructors

Keep the first executable language small. Target roughly 15–25 primitive
constructors, including candidates such as:

- `unit`, `zero`, `combine`, `scale`, `collect`;
- `pair`, `joint`, `restrict`;
- `map`, `compose`, `apply`;
- `extend`, `glue`;
- `quote`, `unquote`, `pack`;
- `view`, `encode`, `decode`;
- `constitute`, `materialise`;
- `project`, `reconstruct`;
- `classify_phase`;
- `evaluate_evidence`;
- `form_diagram`, `close_compatible`;
- `step`, `trace`.

Do not place a constructor in the core merely because an existing profile uses
the word. A primitive belongs in the core only if its typing and semantics are
stable across at least two uses or if it is indispensable to the term language.
Other operations should be library definitions or profile modules.

### 5.4 Equality ladder

Specify separate relations and their permitted implications:

1. syntactic identity;
2. alpha-equivalence where binding exists;
3. canonical identity;
4. definitional equality;
5. denotational equality;
6. typed isomorphism;
7. observational equivalence under an observation family;
8. phase equivalence under `Q`;
9. application-specific substitutability.

No reverse implication is automatic. Every compiler optimisation must name the
equality level it preserves.

## 6. Required semantics

### 6.1 Static semantics

Define:

- grammar and abstract syntax tree;
- binding and substitution;
- signature and edition resolution;
- formation rules for each initial type;
- typing rules for every core constructor;
- effect formation and effect composition;
- constraint admission;
- decidable checks in E7C-B1;
- structured diagnostics.

All examples in the specification must type-check using the implementation or
be marked explicitly as abstract/non-executable.

### 6.2 Dynamic semantics

Choose a small-step or big-step core semantics and justify the choice. A
reference evaluator may use big-step rules initially, but program traces and
noncommuting operations may require a small-step or labelled transition view.

The semantics must expose:

- evaluation order where observable;
- strict versus filtering behaviour;
- partial-domain failure;
- resource-limit interruption;
- witness construction;
- deterministic ordering/canonicalisation;
- retained and excluded alternatives;
- unsupported and undetermined obligations.

### 6.3 Denotational semantics

Give a mathematical interpretation for every implemented type and constructor.
At minimum define:

- the category or collection of admitted carriers;
- interpretations of contexts and substitutions;
- exact-state carriers and coefficient actions;
- partial maps and their composition;
- views and reconstruction fibres;
- quotient/phase semantics;
- finite diagrams and compatible-family closure;
- witness denotations or validity predicates.

If one uniform semantic category is not appropriate, define indexed models or
multiple interpretations connected by explicit adequacy statements.

### 6.4 Observable behaviour

Define program observation before defining optimisation. Candidate observable
components are:

- outcome tag;
- canonical value;
- retained/excluded alternatives;
- effect and loss ledger;
- witness;
- resource consumption within declared abstraction;
- emitted bridge obligations.

Two programs that return the same value but differ between success and failure,
or conceal different loss/evidence effects, are not observationally equivalent
unless a named observation policy intentionally hides that distinction.

## 7. Initial metatheory and proof programme

Each theorem must have a precise statement, hypotheses, proof status and
machine-checking disposition.

### 7.1 Required first theorems

1. **Weakening and exchange**, where permitted by the context discipline.
2. **Substitution:** a well-typed substitution preserves typing and effects.
3. **Canonicalisation soundness:** canonicalisation preserves denotation.
4. **Determinism:** the deterministic E7C-B1 fragment has at most one result.
5. **Preservation:** successful evaluation preserves the declared result type.
6. **Declared progress:** a closed well-typed bounded term yields a value or a
   declared non-success outcome, not an undefined machine state.
7. **Composition soundness:** typed map composition agrees with semantic
   composition under the stated domain conditions.
8. **Projection–reconstruction soundness:** every reported reconstruction
   candidate maps to the selected view.
9. **Quotient descent:** phase-level application is representative-independent
   exactly when domain saturation and result congruence hold.
10. **Witness replay soundness:** checker acceptance implies that the witness
    validates the claimed E7C-B1 derivation under the pinned editions.
11. **Adapter conservativity:** selected profile translations preserve the
    declared bounded source-profile outcomes.
12. **Lowering adequacy:** E7C source evaluation and E7-IR evaluation agree for
    the selected compiler fragment.

### 7.2 Proof status vocabulary

Use only:

- `stated`;
- `proof_sketch`;
- `proved_on_paper`;
- `mechanised`;
- `bounded_exhaustive_check`;
- `counterexample_found`;
- `withdrawn`.

A finite test is not a proof of a universally quantified theorem. A mechanised
proof must name the proof assistant, version, axioms and trusted base.

### 7.3 Mechanisation decision

During the design phase, compare Lean 4, Coq/Rocq and Isabelle/HOL for the
initial core. Select one only after implementing a small representative proof
spike covering substitution, preservation and one partial-map property.

Do not mechanise the entire inherited kernel. Mechanise E7C's formal nucleus
and selected adapter laws.

## 8. Profile adapter programme

### 8.1 EEC-Q adapter

Translate a bounded EEC-Q fragment containing:

- finite configurations;
- exact rational formal states;
- canonical collection and cancellation;
- total and strict partial pushforward;
- independent and joint composition;
- restriction;
- quotation;
- the phase-descent counterexample.

Prove or test preservation of canonical results and failure behaviour.

### 8.2 SF/CFS adapter

Translate:

- finite or finitely described parameter domains;
- constrained realisation;
- pointwise EEC state families;
- shared versus independent parameter dependence;
- empty family versus zero inner state.

Never convert family membership into EEC coefficients.

### 8.3 RGP adapter

Translate a finite subset of:

- layer records;
- hosting and generation;
- quotation and rank;
- source-retaining encoding/decoding;
- projection and contextual expression;
- inheritance;
- composition.

Keep construction rank, scope, time, access and authority independently typed.

### 8.4 WPC adapter

Translate:

- portions and membership;
- contribution and coverage;
- constitution;
- materialisation and representation;
- reconstruction capability;
- proposal versus committed successor where evolution is selected;
- explicit lineage input or deterministic lineage derivation.

Constitution must not be reduced to concatenation, and representation must not
be equated with the semantic whole.

### 8.5 MSC adapter

Translate:

- finite scope diagrams;
- declared scope order;
- common-codomain comparison spans;
- link-wise satisfiability;
- globally compatible families;
- ambiguity and obstruction;
- access quotients and the selected missingness policy;
- scope-state and observation reconstruction fibres.

Preserve the distinction between model coherence and external truth.

### 8.6 REC adapter

Translate:

- claims, evidence items and typed rules;
- independent support and refutation;
- information status;
- scope, temporal and modality guards;
- reliance policy;
- deterministic witness and independent replay.

E7C must not reinterpret EEC coefficients as evidence weights.

### 8.7 Temporal and phase adapter

Translate a bounded fragment of:

- histories and labelled transitions;
- temporal projection and reconstruction;
- operation-order comparison;
- phase criteria;
- representative independence;
- phase-boundary detection.

Do not infer causal reversal from reverse reconstruction or history membership.

### 8.8 Relational non-separability disposition

Evaluate the informative relational non-separability proposal as a candidate
library-level definition over:

- protected whole-properties;
- component views;
- reconstruction fibres;
- retained or missing connecting relations;
- a pinned context, inquiry and equivalence criterion.

The first implementation should attempt to derive its `separable`,
`non_separable` and `undetermined` outcomes from existing E7C operations. Do not
introduce physical, quantum, causal or statistical implications. If the derived
definition is inadequate, record the exact counterexample before proposing a
new core constructor.

## 9. E7-IR/0.1

Define a stable typed intermediate representation after the source calculus is
sufficiently precise. The IR must not become the de facto semantics before the
source semantics is written.

### 9.1 Required properties

- explicit version and capability declaration;
- canonical serialisation;
- immutable signature/profile editions;
- no implicit casts;
- typed operator IDs and domains;
- explicit effects and limits;
- deterministic identifiers;
- structured source locations;
- replayable witnesses;
- forward-compatible rejection of unknown mandatory capabilities.

### 9.2 Minimum instruction families

Candidate instruction groups:

- declaration and binding;
- construction and canonicalisation;
- mapping and restriction;
- family and joint-state operations;
- view/encode/decode;
- quotation and controlled recursion;
- compatible-family closure;
- evidence evaluation;
- phase classification;
- trace and witness emission.

Every instruction requires static typing, dynamic semantics, failure outcomes,
canonical encoding and tests.

## 10. Reference implementation E7C-B1/0.1

### 10.1 Implementation posture

Build a clear reference implementation before optimising. Python is acceptable
for E7C-B1 if the semantics is not accidentally inherited from Python objects,
hashing, exceptions or iteration order. A later implementation may use Rust,
OCaml, Haskell or another language.

### 10.2 Required components

```text
e7c/
  README.md
  pyproject.toml
  src/e7c/
    ast.py
    types.py
    effects.py
    admission.py
    canonical.py
    evaluator.py
    witness.py
    diagnostics.py
    ir.py
    lowering.py
  schemas/
  fixtures/
  tests/
  proofs/
  adapters/
  docs/
```

This is a target layout, not permission to create empty placeholder files. Add
a component only when it contains executable or reviewable substance.

### 10.3 Runtime requirements

- deterministic canonical output;
- controlled diagnostics for all malformed admitted inputs;
- explicit combination, recursion, memory or step limits;
- no network requirement for core evaluation;
- no arbitrary host-language execution;
- no evaluation of untrusted source strings;
- separately implemented witness checker;
- exact arithmetic where the profile requires exactness;
- stable fixture outputs bound by hashes.

### 10.4 Security boundary

Treat parsers, decoders, rule interpreters and witness replay as untrusted-input
boundaries. Include malformed nested structures, duplicate IDs, oversized
inputs, cycles, deep acyclic graphs, missing editions and unsupported
capabilities in the negative suite.

## 11. Validation strategy

### 11.1 Test layers

| Layer | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Parser/schema | accepted and rejected interchange documents |
| Type system | positive and negative derivation cases |
| Evaluator | every constructor and every outcome tag |
| Algebraic laws | property-based tests within bounded generators |
| Counterexamples | deliberate failures of overbroad laws |
| Witness checker | valid, tampered and edition-mismatched witnesses |
| Adapters | source-profile fixture equivalence |
| Compiler | source/IR differential tests |
| Resource limits | deterministic interruption without validity changes |
| Application | end-to-end decision-relevant cases |

### 11.2 Required counterexamples

Maintain executable examples showing that:

- equal output does not imply equal computation;
- cancellation cannot hide domain failure before admission;
- a view need not reconstruct a unique source;
- phase-equivalent inputs can fail to support a phase operation;
- link-wise satisfiability need not imply global compatibility;
- a representation can be stale while its prior reconstruction is valid;
- support and refutation can coexist;
- identical final states can arise from materially different histories;
- two individually valid rewrites can fail to commute;
- a resource limit is not mathematical incompatibility.

### 11.3 Continuous integration

Every E7C PR must run:

- formatting and static checks;
- schema validation;
- unit and integration tests;
- property tests with pinned seeds where used;
- fixture replay;
- witness verification;
- hash/manifest verification;
- documentation link and example checks;
- proof build after a proof assistant is selected.

No validation record may state a check passed unless the corresponding CI or
reproduction command exists and was run against the recorded source commit.

### 11.4 REC-EVAL/0.1 relationship

REC-EVAL/0.1 is the current proposed empirical gate for REC-assisted reasoning.
E7C must not copy benchmark results into calculus laws or optimise its semantics
against synthetic fixtures. When Pilot A begins, review and, if admitted,
version-pin REC-EVAL's case-set, run, blinding, adjudication and scoring
contracts. Preserve its comparison between retrieval plus REC and the same
retrieval interface without REC. Report null results, failures, excessive
abstention, latency and cost.

Until PR #31 is reviewed and merged, treat its contents as an open proposal,
not repository-wide policy.

## 12. Application pilots

The foundation must be tested through applications, but application demand
must not dictate unsound core semantics.

### Pilot A — reasoning and translation assurance

Use a bounded version of the existing typed-clause/REC workflow:

1. admit independently extracted source and target structures;
2. type modality, actor, action, object, conditions and exceptions;
3. compare preservation under a pinned domain profile;
4. retain supporting, refuting, missing and conflicting evidence;
5. emit an independently replayable witness;
6. abstain when extraction or domain rules are unsupported.

Measure errors caught, false alarms, abstention, latency, cost and expert review
time against the existing baseline.

### Pilot B — modular software/configuration assembly

Represent components, interfaces, dependency constraints, versions, proposed
local changes, admitted assemblies and generated artefacts. Demonstrate stale
artefact detection and distinguish component validity from globally compatible
assembly.

### Pilot C — E7Q/E7Q-IR bridge

Only after the classical E7C core is stable, specify a typed adapter to E7Q-IR.
Do not describe rational EEC-Q states as quantum amplitudes. A quantum adapter
must supply complex state semantics, permitted operations, measurement and a
correctness relation to the target quantum IR.

At least Pilot A must reach a reproducible end-to-end result before considering
E7C kernel promotion.

### 14.3 PR sizing

Prefer one work package or one coherent semantic decision per PR. A PR must
state:

- problem and scope;
- formal change;
- executable change;
- proof/change obligations;
- compatibility impact;
- tests and reproduction commands;
- unsupported claims;
- reviewer questions.

### 14.4 Review roles

Seek distinct review perspectives:

- originating E7G-T author/fidelity review;
- mathematical semantics review;
- programming-languages/type-systems review;
- implementation/security review;
- application-domain review for pilots.

One AI's repeated self-review is first-party review, even if separate model
instances are used. Record it accurately.

### 14.5 Change control

The implementing AI may correct clear implementation defects autonomously on
its branch. It must request a decision before:

- changing the meaning of an existing profile;
- adding or removing a core equality;
- selecting one of materially different partiality semantics;
- changing witness trust boundaries;
- modifying canonical-kernel text;
- claiming a theorem, empirical advantage or product readiness;
- splitting repositories or changing licensing.

## 16. Risk register seed

| Risk | Early control |
| --- | --- |
| Profile accumulation continues under a new name | freeze core constructors during WP2–WP4 |
| Category-theoretic terminology outruns definitions | require explicit objects, morphisms and laws |
| Type system becomes undecidable | isolate refinements and require bounded decidable B1 checks |
| Effects become an unstructured metadata list | define composition and observational consequences |
| Host-language behaviour leaks into semantics | canonical data model and differential tests |
| Proof claims rely on tests | mandatory proof-status field |
| IR defines semantics accidentally | source semantics precedes lowering |
| Adapters erase source distinctions | anti-collapse and conservativity tests |
| Resource limits change document validity | validate before bounded evaluation |
| Recursion creates nontermination | guarded/bounded fragment and explicit fuel |
| Witness checker trusts evaluator output | independent recomputation from source and editions |
| AI generates internally consistent but unusable formalisms | end-to-end Pilot A and external review |
| Product pressure distorts mathematics | separate research and application gates |
| Speculation is presented as empirical science | explicit bridge and evidence requirements |

## 17. Immediate first build cycle

The implementing AI should start with the following bounded cycle, not with the
entire roadmap at once.

### Step 1 — Establish WP0

Create `research/e7c-0.1/` and the source, decision, risk and current-state
registers. Pin the exact hashes/commits for:

- v0.12.1/RGP2;
- EEC-Q, SF and CFS companions;
- v0.13/WPC candidate and package;
- v0.14/REC candidate and package;
- MSC/0.1 merged proposal;
- REC-EVAL/0.1 open evaluation package and exact PR head;
- the open relational non-separability companion and its status;
- relevant E7Q/E7Q-IR interfaces, without importing quantum semantics.

### Step 2 — Produce the minimal charter

Define:

- purpose;
- non-goals;
- initial term boundary;
- judgement forms;
- equality ladder;
- outcome taxonomy;
- effect candidates;
- ten highest-risk semantic decisions.

### Step 3 — Implement one vertical slice

Use a deliberately small program:

1. admit two finite configurations;
2. form an exact rational state;
3. apply a strict partial map;
4. create a lossy view;
5. compute its reconstruction candidates;
6. classify candidates under a phase criterion;
7. return an explicit outcome, loss ledger and witness;
8. replay the witness independently.

This slice exercises construction, partiality, projection, reconstruction,
phase and evidence of execution without requiring every profile.

### Step 4 — State and test the first laws

State canonicalisation soundness, deterministic evaluation, preservation and
projection–reconstruction soundness for the vertical slice. Add deliberate
counterexamples for invalid phase descent and hidden partial-map failure.

### Step 5 — Open the first implementation PR

The PR must remain labelled as E7C research. Request review of the judgements,
equality ladder, partiality semantics and witness trust boundary before adding
more constructors.

## 18. Instructions for the supervising/review AI

The supervising AI following this work on GitHub must:

1. compare each PR with this mandate and the pinned source profiles;
2. inspect code, tests, schemas, manifests and proof claims—not only summaries;
3. reproduce bounded tests where possible;
4. challenge every new primitive, implicit coercion and equality;
5. identify divergences between prose, schema, evaluator and witness checker;
6. distinguish defects from legitimate research choices;
7. propose exact replacement text or code behaviour for findings;
8. avoid expanding scope while unresolved foundational defects remain;
9. ensure canonical status and unsupported claims remain accurate;
10. approve, request corrections or recommend stopping a work package using
    explicit evidence.

The supervising AI may suggest modifications to this roadmap through a focused
PR. It must not treat the roadmap as immutable if implementation reveals a real
contradiction, but every change must retain provenance and explain its impact.

## 19. Stop conditions

Pause expansion and request a design decision if:

- the same term requires incompatible meanings across adapters;
- a proposed equality changes failure or loss behaviour;
- type checking requires unrestricted theorem proving;
- witness replay cannot be separated from evaluator trust;
- resource-bounded and abstract semantics are being conflated;
- an adapter requires changing its source profile;
- a claimed theorem has a counterexample;
- a new feature lacks an application, proof or semantic necessity;
- implementation complexity grows without a completed vertical slice;
- canonical promotion is proposed before independent review and reproduction.

Stopping is a valid research outcome. Do not conceal an obstruction by adding
terminology.

## 20. Final instruction

Begin with WP0 and the minimal vertical slice. Preserve the speculative ambition
of E7G-T, but make every executable commitment typed, denotationally interpretable,
operationally explicit, testable and reviewable.

The desired result is not merely a larger document. It is a calculus in which:

- valid constructions can be expressed;
- invalid compositions are rejected;
- loss and uncertainty remain visible;
- equivalent programs are equivalent for stated reasons;
- local and global structures can be related without conflation;
- results carry checkable provenance; and
- domain-specific languages and applications can compile through a stable,
  bounded formal foundation.

Do not promote E7C by aspiration. Build the evidence that would make promotion
a reasoned future decision.
